# Cheras - Evidence-Judgment Divergence, misses and forward review

Locked on 6 October 2026 after initial desk research. This is case work, not Core. No new gate or causal module is created. Sources are in [the evidence register](Cheras_Evidence_Register.md); decisions remain in [G0-G9](Cheras_G0_G9_Cases.md).

A flag identifies a material conflict in the investment interpretation of evidence for a specified buyer and horizon. Different facts can coexist without conflict: rent and family preference are not opposite measurements. Where those facts are used to justify an ownership premium, the inferential bridge is what is challenged. Unknown information alone does not create a flag. No flag below licenses Deploy.

## DF01 - M Vertica: accessible low quantum versus exit interchangeability

⚠ DIVERGENCE FLAG

**Observed Evidence:** E15 offers three bedrooms/850 sf at RM489k; E24 gives a whole-unit rental asking lead. These support an initial affordability/use interpretation. E04 also establishes substantial same-development supply. None proves a realised return.

**Contradicting Judgment / Observation:** My concern is that numerous close substitutes and operating friction may prevent the five-year resale premium needed by the investment, despite a usable location and attractive entry quantum. The proposition in dispute is whether this specific product can retain financeable owner demand at the required exit, not whether central access exists.

**Possible Explanations:**

- A. Data Limitation: listing terms, furnishing, parking, tower and primary/resale mixture may explain the apparent price attraction; project medians mix periods and products.
- B. Judgment Bias: density prejudice could cause me to reject useful affordable housing; a city-access narrative could cause the opposite error. Test both.
- C. Omitted Variable: lift-zone load, gate-to-platform route, parking journey, tenancy controls and practical three-bedroom use may differentiate units despite a common project label.
- D. Structural Change: new nearby supply could intensify competition; better local services or sustained owner adoption could instead deepen demand. Announcements alone establish neither effect.

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** At a sufficiently disciplined entry, the compact three-bedroom use case can support independent owner and rental-investor demand; the apparent low quantum is not wholly compensation for an intolerable functional defect. No appreciation rate is assumed.

**Expected Confirming Evidence:** Repeated, matched voluntary resales to identifiable ownership users; whole-unit tenancies on defensible terms; acceptable observed circulation; no reliance on a promotional price unavailable to ordinary buyers.

**Evidence Required:** Exact unit rights and condition, tower/type sale history, deduplicated competing units, actual net rental package and operating evidence.

**Falsification Conditions:** Advertised entry requires unavailable conditions; mandatory parking/use needs fail; owner buyers consistently choose functional substitutes after adjustment; rent only works through a materially different room/short-stay business; or matched competition prevents the exit economics from being supported.

**Core Module Routing:** G0/G2/G3/G4/G5/G6/G7; Comparable Set, Buyer Universe, Operational Density, Owner Conversion, Exit Liquidity and Financing Risk.

**Current Status:** Unresolved. Potential hidden structural risk and a countervailing possible value opportunity both remain open.

**Decision Consequence:** C01 Defer, first investigation tier; no low-price or density shortcut. Uncertainty reduces conviction and increases evidence/protection requirements. No numerical margin of safety exists until G0 is established.

**Next Review:** When exact-unit evidence or new matched sales/tenancies become available. Outcome unobserved; no automatic monitoring created.

## DF02 - Riana South: rental appeal versus family resale premium

⚠ DIVERGENCE FLAG

**Observed Evidence:** Whole-unit rental asks and UCSI's presence support plausible accommodation demand. The selected furnished unit is offered at RM595k. E09/E20/E29/E37.

**Contradicting Judgment / Observation:** I do not yet accept the interpretation that rental strength justifies a durable family ownership premium at the model's future exit quantum. The contested proposition is the transfer from occupancy usefulness to resale willingness-to-pay, not the existence of tenants.

**Possible Explanations:**

- A. Data Limitation: asking versus achieved rent, furnishing/parking differences, room advertisements and mixed-size transaction samples can inflate apparent support.
- B. Judgment Bias: green-product/brand appeal may encourage a preferred family thesis; conversely an excessive student-housing stereotype may hide actual ownership desirability.
- C. Omitted Variable: original layout, resident routines, family privacy, management, parking and buyer motives could explain independent ownership demand.
- D. Structural Change: new accommodation alternatives, institutional residences, changing campus demand or local household adoption can change the balance after the historical sample.

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** The selected unit has a credible rental use case, but a material family resale premium requires independently observable ownership adoption; rental demand alone is insufficient to explain the required exit.

**Expected Confirming Evidence:** Rental activity alongside owner-buyer resistance at higher quantum, or ownership bids that track income value rather than a distinct family premium.

**Evidence Required:** Matched voluntary resale terms and buyer motives; actual whole-unit lease packages; original layouts and operating controls; same-weekend alternatives at the proposed exit budget.

**Falsification Conditions:** Repeated comparable owner-occupier purchases support a sustained premium after adjusting condition, parking and financing, with credible product-specific reasons independent of rental income. That would disprove the current weak-transfer concern and raise its rank.

**Core Module Routing:** G1/G2/G3/G5/G6; Usage versus Ownership, Demand Decomposition, Buyer Conversion, Asset Fit and Exit Buyer.

**Current Status:** Unresolved; possible hidden structural risk in the premium interpretation. No proven structural failure.

**Decision Consequence:** C06 Defer, second tier. Do not increase assumed rent or substitute a family buyer narrative to rescue return. Require additional evidence and a defensible entry cushion.

**Next Review:** A matched lease/resale and buyer-use package; no matured outcome.

## DF03 - Windows large: low PSF and quality versus total exit quantum

⚠ DIVERGENCE FLAG

**Observed Evidence:** The selected 2,497-sf ask equates to roughly RM360 psf; reported governance evidence is positive. Mixed project-price data can make this look like a discounted quality asset. E22/E35/E40.

**Contradicting Judgment / Observation:** My judgment is that much of the low PSF may compensate for a large absolute budget and a narrower future buyer pool. A household's preference for the project is not proof it will pay the investor's required future quantum. This is also a potential hidden-mispricing investigation: positive quality judgment could prove the apparent size discount excessive, but that has not been demonstrated.

**Possible Explanations:**

- A. Data Limitation: comparing different unit sizes, parking, renovation and strata share entitlements manufactures discounts; large-unit rent is weakly evidenced.
- B. Judgment Bias: park, freehold, award or large-space preferences can overstate willingness-to-pay; a landed-first preference can wrongly dismiss a legitimate strata-service buyer.
- C. Omitted Variable: the value of single-level space, managed landscape, security, three usable bays and maintenance convenience must be weighed against charges and capital obligations.
- D. Structural Change: improved governance could strengthen reputation; ageing systems, future works and changes in alternative family housing could offset that advantage.

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** The apparent low PSF primarily reflects size/quantum segmentation rather than a recoverable discount to matched large-unit clearing. A specialised family buyer can still prefer the asset, but does not establish the investment's exit price.

**Expected Confirming Evidence:** Large units show thinner matched buyer depth or lower PSF than medium units; the apparent discount substantially disappears after proper size/condition/rights adjustment.

**Evidence Required:** Comparable large-unit voluntary transactions and marketing histories, buyer motives, actual share-unit charges, works funding and achieved whole-unit rents; condition-adjusted landed/large-condo alternatives as diagnostic choices.

**Falsification Conditions:** Several independent matched transactions establish a genuine gap for the selected unit after costs and defects, with sufficiently deep buyers demonstrably choosing this service package at the relevant quantum. Rising small-unit median PSF or an award alone cannot falsify the hypothesis.

**Core Module Routing:** G0/G2/G3/G5/G6/G7/G8; Comparable Set, Absolute Price Ceiling, Governance, Owner-Occupier Desirability, Exit Liquidity and Capital Allocation.

**Current Status:** Partially Resolved. The mixed-median discount inference is rejected; exact large-unit value and future adoption remain unresolved. Governance evidence improves a separate question and does not resolve valuation.

**Decision Consequence:** C08 Preserve Capacity. Better governance does not remove the model's marginal reserve headroom. Investigate a smaller layout as a new case, not by changing this case after the result.

**Next Review:** Matched large-unit data or a separately identified smaller unit; no realised investment outcome.

## DF04 - Aster family capability versus incremental price premium

⚠ DIVERGENCE FLAG

**Observed Evidence:** Official layout evidence and the user's earlier market judgment support family capability within an investment-oriented TOD. B and C differ in utility and quantum. The current Maxim ask gives a larger advertised family-format alternative. E08/E18/E19/E43.

**Contradicting Judgment / Observation:** The user's earlier objection was that the higher family-format total price can approach Cheras landed options. My extension is that B also has to justify its price/space trade-off against actual condo substitutes. Neither objection disproves family capability; they challenge the premium inferred from it.

**Possible Explanations:**

- A. Data Limitation: layouts, parking, renovations, tenure and realistic landed acquisition/refurbishment costs differ; raw asking gaps are not willingness-to-pay measures.
- B. Judgment Bias: preference for balconies, extra bathrooms or landed homes may be mistaken for a general market rule; station enthusiasm can create the opposite bias.
- C. Omitted Variable: route quality, practical room use, privacy, parking geometry and household travel needs may earn a defensible premium.
- D. Structural Change: new competing supply, post-VP ownership adoption or improved surrounding routes may alter the reference set. Existing MRT presence alone is not new recognition.

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** Family capability is real, but the extra utility earns an investment premium only when matched owner demand supports the additional total cost and future exit; otherwise the lower-quantum expression has the stronger investment entry case.

**Expected Confirming Evidence:** Limited incremental owner bids or rent for the more expensive type; same households choose a cheaper functional condo or viable landed alternative after full-cost adjustment.

**Evidence Required:** Matched B/C transactions, authentic unit/parking condition, ownership motives and substitute choices at the relevant budget. Campus renters alone do not answer this.

**Falsification Conditions:** Repeated voluntary family purchases demonstrate a persistent incremental premium attributable to the extra utility, after condition, parking and financing adjustment, sufficient to compensate for the added investment capital.

**Core Module Routing:** G0/G2/G3/G4/G5/G6; Asset Fit, Buyer Universe, Functional Substitution, Absolute Price Ceiling and Owner Conversion.

**Current Status:** Unresolved. Family capability is retained; investment premium remains unproved.

**Decision Consequence:** C04 Defer; B currently preferred for investigation over an unproved C premium. No permanent anti-C or anti-TOD rule. Unknown G0 prevents inventing a fixed percentage margin of safety.

**Next Review:** New matched type-level evidence. Origin explicitly retrospective to the user's Phase 7 correction; not scored as an independent blind discovery.

## Case-level SOP improvements

Apply identity checks before price sorting: project aliases, address, tower/type, room count, area and title class must agree. Preserve conflicting fields rather than choosing the attractive one. Separate portal listing refresh, original marketing date, registered transaction date and retrieval date. Deduplicate by actual parcel where possible; otherwise report sample coverage as unknown.

Keep rent package and buyer use distinct. Whole-unit and room/short-stay income cannot be silently substituted. Match parking and furnishing, and retain a realistic fit-out allowance. Use actual strata share entitlements for charges; never apply a per-share-unit fee as a per-square-foot rate.

Every rank change needs its triggering evidence and remaining falsifiers. A positive governance update cannot repair an unrelated valuation gap. A source correction can resolve a measurement error without establishing mispricing. These are execution controls under existing Core; no Core promotion is proposed.
