# Residential Investment Framework

Single consolidated master for reusable residential investment principles, decision rules and analytical controls. The market-causal Core is frozen; the embedded Evidence–Judgment Divergence control governs investigation across the unchanged G0–G9 process. This master contains no populated cases, area analyses, backtests, predictions or conversation logs. Existing section identifiers are retained for stable mechanism routing; numbering gaps do not denote additional gates or missing required stages.

Evidence, contextual factor roles and identifiable mechanisms determine the decision. Alignment, analyst seniority and personal conviction never replace required underwriting evidence. Scope preferences remain contextual, not universal exclusion rules. The four G9 capital decisions remain Deploy, Defer, Reject and Preserve Capacity.

The complete definitions, investigation tests, routing and reusable flag contract are embedded under the Core's cross-module decision control. Operational notes and historical records remain outside the master.

# 2. User's Core Investment Philosophy

The user's core lens is:

> **"I want the best self-occupied condo in the most happening area."**

Operational meaning:

1. Identify the genuinely vibrant / convenient / desirable urban node first.
2. Then choose the best owner-occupation product inside that node.
3. Do not optimise only for:
   - highest rental yield;
   - cheapest PSF;
   - biggest discount;
   - shortest MRT distance.
4. Prioritise:
   - livability;
   - layout efficiency;
   - access;
   - privacy;
   - noise;
   - product quality;
   - resident profile;
   - management;
   - owner-occupier appeal;
   - future exit quality.

The user also seeks **mispricing / special situations**:
- stigma;
- distressed sellers;
- VP shock;
- abandoned-project stigma;
- temporary supply release;
- price discovery errors;
- recoverable discounts.

The goal is often to use mispricing to buy a better / larger owner-occupier product at a lower-tier price.

However:

> Cheapness alone is not alpha.

The system must distinguish:
- defensive entry;
- recoverable discount;
- structural discount;
- true compounding quality.

### Evidence–Judgment Divergence Principle — cross-module decision control

**Architectural status:** epistemic / decision-control layer inside the master framework. It is not a market causal module, an investment role, a new capital decision or an additional gate. The existing 35 Core principles and G0–G9 remain authoritative. Candidate mechanisms remain candidates. A flag can activate at any relevant stage without changing the order or definitions of existing gates.

This control specifies how competing explanations are investigated; it does not assert a newly validated market-causal mechanism. The existing promotion threshold remains unchanged for any proposed causal addition. New execution refinements belong in SOP unless the existing promotion requirements are met.

`Evidence → Divergence Check → Interpretation → Existing Core Mechanisms → Investment Decision`

**Evidence–Judgment Divergence Principle:** When observed market evidence conflicts with informed market judgment, the divergence itself is treated as an analytical signal, not as a basis for overriding the evidence. A material divergence must trigger further investigation. It does not constitute an investment thesis by itself.

Required sequence:

`Divergence → Investigation → Falsifiable Hypothesis → Additional Evidence → Existing Core Mechanism → Decision`

Prohibited shortcut:

`Divergence → Trust intuition → Invest`

**Safeguards**

> Divergence is a trigger for investigation, not permission to override evidence.

> An investment thesis must ultimately be explainable through an identifiable market mechanism rather than personal conviction alone.

> Strong conviction without a falsifiable mechanism increases risk rather than analytical confidence.

#### Definition, evidence units and materiality

`Informed Market Judgment = Pattern Recognition + Market Experience + Micro-Market Observation + Comparable Knowledge + Product Understanding + Buyer Behaviour Recognition`

The formal term is **Evidence–Judgment Divergence**. Informal intuition is acceptable as an anomaly-detection prompt; instinctive guessing, authority, confidence and personal taste are not substitutes for informed judgment or evidence. Record the experience, observations and comparable knowledge supporting the judgment. Attribute practitioner input and distinguish direct observation from interpretation.

Define the proposition being tested, asset/market scope, relevant buyer, lifecycle, time horizon and proposed decision before classifying polarity. Positive and Negative refer to support for that same proposition, not an asset-wide good/bad score. Positive rental performance and negative ownership appeal can both be true; the material conflict may be with the interpretation that one guarantees the other. Explain that inference rather than pretending the raw observations contradict each other.

Trigger **⚠ DIVERGENCE FLAG** only when the discrepancy could materially change valuation, entry decision, required margin of safety, asset ranking, exit liquidity, downside risk or conviction level. Explain what could change. Trivial differences need no flag. Missing or weak evidence is UNKNOWN, not automatically Negative; absence alone does not invent a conflict. An independent material qualitative observation can nevertheless trigger investigation when the quantitative series is incomplete.

Do not select a different scope or horizon merely to make disagreement disappear. Record whether apparently conflicting claims concern different units, cohorts, price types, dates or decision questions; validate any proposed explanation for that mismatch.

#### Four-state logic

| Observed Evidence | Informed Judgment | Interpretation | Default Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive | Positive | Evidence aligned | Continue normal underwriting; alignment does not pass an untested gate |
| Negative | Negative | Negative alignment | Usually reject / deprioritise; use G9 Reject only for an established structural Gate failure, otherwise retain the applicable existing decision |
| Negative | Positive | Possible hidden mispricing or omitted variable | Trigger Divergence Investigation; no presumption that weak evidence is wrong |
| Positive | Negative | Possible hidden structural risk | Trigger Divergence Investigation with at least equal attention; no presumption that the judgment is wrong |

The four states are analytical states, not four additional investment decisions. Unknown evidence or unsupported judgment remains explicitly unclassified until interpretable; do not create a false positive/negative label to fill the table. Neither side wins by default. Agreement can still reflect shared bias or a shared erroneous source.

#### Automatic checks throughout underwriting

At initial evidence intake, each relevant gate, any material new observation and before the final capital decision, compare:

- Quantitative Evidence vs Qualitative Evidence.
- Historical Evidence vs Forward-Looking Market Structure.
- Location Strength vs Project Strength.
- Rental Performance vs Owner-Occupier Desirability.
- Headline PSF vs True Comparable Value.
- Reported Liquidity vs Real Exit Buyer Depth.
- Current Weakness vs Temporary Lifecycle Distortion.
- Current Strength vs Potential Structural Deterioration.

Record a brief no-material-divergence result in the case workpaper when appropriate; do not generate a warning merely to complete a checklist. Any judgment relied on for a valuation or investment claim must be expressed as a falsifiable proposition, whether or not a material flag is needed. Keep all populated flags and evidence outside this master.

#### Investigation: four competing explanation families

Every material flag must test **all four families A–D**, including the analyst's own error. They can coexist; they are not a forced single-choice taxonomy. For each record the hypothesis, best supporting and opposing evidence, remaining unknowns and conclusion. If a family is inapplicable, explain why. Unsupported is not disproven, and an investigation request is not additional evidence.

**A. Data Limitation**

Investigate whether observed evidence is distorted by:

- transaction-data lag;
- low transaction volume;
- small sample size;
- poor comparable selection;
- non-like-for-like comparables;
- lifecycle effects;
- VP-stage distortion;
- temporary supply release;
- unrecorded incentives;
- developer rebates;
- furnishing packages;
- aggregation error;
- stale asking-price data;
- valuation lag;
- incomplete rental evidence;
- unusual seller composition.

Retain the original observations, record proposed exclusions/adjustments and trace source lineage. Repeated portal copies do not create independent corroboration. Correct a measurement problem only with an evidenced reason, then rerun the affected existing gate. A corrected dataset is not automatically evidence of undervaluation.

**B. Judgment Bias**

Actively attempt to falsify the analyst's own preferred explanation. Test:

- ownership bias;
- anchoring;
- confirmation bias;
- familiarity bias;
- recency bias;
- narrative attachment;
- personal lifestyle preference;
- aesthetic preference;
- sunk-cost thinking;
- desire to justify a predetermined entry price.

State the strongest rival account and what would cause withdrawal of the preferred interpretation. Apply the same evidential burden to attractive and unattractive conclusions. Research effort, ownership, prior endorsement or practitioner seniority cannot lower that burden. Do not ask a practitioner to supply the desired conclusion before forming an independent view.

**C. Omitted Variable**

Investigate whether the measured evidence misses a decision-relevant variable:

- owner-occupier desirability;
- buyer-pool depth;
- tenant profile;
- product positioning;
- layout efficiency;
- privacy;
- lift performance;
- circulation quality;
- drop-off experience;
- parking usability;
- management quality;
- maintenance quality;
- noise;
- view quality;
- pedestrian environment;
- retail-interface quality;
- neighbourhood perception;
- external negative adjacency;
- financing constraints;
- supply composition;
- competing future supply;
- micro-location accessibility;
- catalyst recognition;
- social / prestige signalling;
- functional substitution set.

Specify how the variable could change actual buyer behaviour, utility, financeability, clearing price or exit. Describing an attractive feature is insufficient. Test its incremental explanation against true substitutes and existing Core mechanisms; do not invent a weight or a universal screening rule.

**D. Structural Change Not Yet Reflected in Historical Data**

Test for genuine change through:

- new infrastructure;
- changed accessibility;
- emerging employment nodes;
- new commercial ecosystem;
- changing resident demographics;
- neighbourhood upgrading;
- supply exhaustion;
- regeneration;
- planning changes;
- new buyer pool;
- changed financing conditions;
- post-VP normalisation;
- stigma recovery;
- improved management;
- major amenity completion.

Historical data must not automatically be treated as forward-looking truth. Equally, announced change is not delivered utility or demonstrated buyer willingness-to-pay. Distinguish proposed, funded, under construction, operating and adopted stages where relevant; verify the transmission to the selected asset and future buyer. Test delay, non-delivery, competing supply and the possibility that the change is already priced in. Neither pessimism nor optimism exempts a forward thesis from falsification.

#### Convert judgment into a falsifiable hypothesis

Do not retain “I feel this project is undervalued” or “The project does not feel right” as the explanation. Before further interpretation, specify:

- the exact proposition and the preferred explanation;
- the causal link to an existing mechanism, with asset/buyer scope and horizon;
- expected confirming observations, including what should change and relative to which baseline/substitutes;
- expected falsifying observations and the strongest rival explanation;
- evidence to obtain, its source and quality/independence requirements;
- measurement period or event trigger and a review deadline justified by the mechanism;
- decision consequences if confirmed, falsified or still unresolved.

Evidence should discriminate between rival explanations, not merely repeat the original observation. Predeclare tests before seeing the new outcome where feasible; record later hypothesis amendments rather than moving the goalposts. Do not invent numerical thresholds when the available evidence cannot support them.

For a **temporary supply-distortion hypothesis**, confirming candidates include falling deduplicated active listings, narrowing matched resale discounts, better rental absorption, stabilising occupancy, fewer distressed sellers and improving genuine transaction depth. Falsifiers include persistent inventory beyond the justified normalisation window, weak owner-occupier demand, widening gaps to true substitutes, poor financing acceptance and worsening absorption. Falling listings alone may reflect withdrawals; improving rents alone may support usage without ownership premium. Recoverable mispricing still requires valid reference prices and reversibility under 5.30 and G5.

For an **owner-occupier willingness-to-pay weakness hypothesis**, test resale spreads against true substitutes, genuine transaction velocity, evidenced owner-occupier ratio, actual buyer profiles, layout/circulation/privacy/noise, governance, externalities and repeat resale behaviour. Seek counterevidence of stable matched resale preference and financeable owner-buyer depth. A subjective design preference or asserted resident ratio does not validate a resale ceiling.

These are reusable hypothesis forms and evidence tests, not historical cases or universal predictive indicators.

#### Symmetric investment implications

**Potential Hidden Mispricing:** observed data is weak while informed judgment is positive. Temporary distortion, an omitted variable or delayed recognition may explain the gap. It becomes an investable thesis only if evidence establishes the explanation, valid clearing/entry basis, recoverable share, acceptable asset/financing risks, credible exit and suitable capital allocation. Weak data plus positive conviction is not an investment role.

**Potential Hidden Structural Risk:** observed data is positive while informed judgment is negative. Historical strength may coexist with a future exit-value ceiling, narrowing buyer pool, resale weakness, owner-occupier resistance, structural externality, product obsolescence or lifecycle deterioration. Give this state at least as much investigative attention as possible undervaluation. Good headline metrics cannot average away an independently established critical Gate failure. Negative judgment alone cannot establish that failure.

#### Unresolved divergence, safety margin and decision precedence

`Unresolved Divergence → Higher Uncertainty → Higher Required Margin of Safety → Lower Position Conviction → More Evidence Required`

Record the increase in required protection relative to the no-divergence case: a more conservative supported value/exit assumption, a lower justified entry ceiling, additional cash/repair/forced-hold buffers or reduced permitted exposure as appropriate. Explain the mechanism and basis; no universal percentage haircut or numerical conviction score is imposed. Do not count the same uncertainty twice without justification.

If normalised clearing is unknown, do not fabricate a numerical valuation margin or validated discount. State that the required margin is higher but cannot yet be responsibly quantified. A lower offered price, smaller position or larger reserve cannot cure an unpassed critical gate or turn a structural failure into a deployable asset.

All flags retain **Resolved / Partially Resolved / Unresolved** status:

- **Resolved:** discriminating evidence validates or rejects the proposed explanation, the finding is routed and all affected decisions/exit implications are re-evaluated. Resolution may establish a bad investment; it is not a synonym for Deploy.
- **Partially Resolved:** some explanations or evidence problems are resolved, but material residual uncertainty remains. List the unresolved components and maintain their higher proof/safety burden.
- **Unresolved:** no sufficiently supported explanation. Lower conviction, seek the specified evidence and preserve the affected gate stop. Repeated waiting or a favourable price move does not resolve it.

Review status at G9 and on material new evidence; reopen a resolved flag when its premise ceases to hold. Retain earlier hypotheses, dates, evidence and changes in the case register outside this master. Avoid circular closure based solely on analyst confidence or user agreement.

Decision precedence remains G0–G9: insufficient evidence or an unresolved required thesis/gate ordinarily yields **Defer**; an independently established structural Gate failure supports **Reject**; an independently established allocation constraint supports **Preserve Capacity**. **Deploy** still requires sufficient evidence and asset plus portfolio gates passing. Residual uncertainty, if compatible with those standards, must be explicit and the thesis must survive its plausible adverse explanation; never waive a gate using a flag or safety margin. Negative alignment does not invent a structural failure, and divergence resolution does not auto-deploy.

## 5.1 Context-Adaptive Factor Importance

Do not use universal weights.

Use:

`Factor Importance = f(Context, Buyer, Product, Lifecycle)`

Factors can function as:
- Driver;
- Gate;
- Floor;
- Ceiling;
- Modifier.

Sequence:

`Context -> Future Buyer -> Dominant Mechanism -> Drivers/Gates/Floors/Ceilings -> Decision`

A failed critical Gate cannot be averaged away by many positives.

## 5.2 Property Value Causal Chain

Core causal chain:

`Location + Product Positioning`
-> `Resident Profile`
-> `Governance / Management`
-> `Lived Environment`
-> `Resale Reputation`
-> `Price Stability / Appreciation`

Immutable design matters:
- lift ratio;
- corridor / circulation;
- parking;
- drop-off;
- refuse;
- loading;
- noise;
- layout;
- privacy.

Management can preserve or destroy premium.

Management cannot turn an ordinary area into a premium address by itself.

## 5.3 Premium / Vibe as Revealed Preference

"Vibe" can be economically real if it repeatedly changes willingness-to-pay.

Look for:
- repeated higher benchmarks;
- take-up despite cheaper substitutes;
- post-completion persistence;
- higher buyer willingness-to-pay.

Distinguish:
- durable premium;
- launch hype;
- collective preference;
- temporary narrative.

Buyer breadth and buyer depth are different.

---

## 5.4 Demographic / Identity Orientation

Do not treat ethnicity as intrinsic value.

Translate it into:
- buyer-pool depth;
- purchasing power;
- community network;
- commercial / social ecosystem;
- location loyalty;
- intergenerational attachment;
- cross-segment acceptance.

## 5.5 Intergenerational / Location-First Demand

Mature areas can have address-first buyers.

- landed loyalty -> strata loyalty;

`First High-Rise = Market Experiment, not Township Maturity`

- multiple vertical generations;

- launch timing;

`Premium Persistence != Premium Growth`

## 5.8 Supply

Use:

`Effective Competing Supply`
or
`Core-Substitutable Supply`

- raise benchmark;

- segment buyer pools.

## 5.9 Premium Formation / Overheat

Healthy premium markets still discriminate:
- good;
- average;
- bad.

Warning sign:
all catalyst-linked projects are chased indiscriminately.

Catalysts can create demand, but also narratives.

## 5.10 Dynamic Price Ceiling

`Capital Appreciation Ceiling_t`
=
`Future Reference Set_t`
+
`Prime Scarcity Premium_t`
+
`Durable Consensus Premium_t`
-
`Structural Penalty_t`

Current reference set != future reference set.

Absolute quantum matters.

Buyer asks:

> What else can I buy with the same total budget?

## 5.11 PSF vs Absolute Quantum

Small units can carry high PSF while staying under budget thresholds.

Use:

`Price Ceiling = Next Best Use of Buyer's Budget`

Do not infer premium from PSF alone.

- `P_Secondary Clearing`
- `P_Auction Reserve`
- `P_Auction Sold`
- `P_Asking`

`Developer Price - Acquisition Price`

a discount automatically.

`Normalised Clearing Price`

Then:

`Validated Discount = Normalised Clearing Price - Acquisition Price`

Then:

`Recoverable Discount = Validated Discount × Reversible Share`

Phrase:

> Price history doesn't create value. Buyer depth creates value.

---

## 5.31 Pure Own-Stay vs Multi-Anchor Location Demand

A wealthy landed area may be valuable because residents want landed housing, not because the location supports all housing forms.

Distinguish:

### Pure Residential / Housing-Form Loyalty

vs

### Multi-Anchor Location Demand

Anchors may include:
- employment;
- education;
- healthcare;
- commercial;
- transport;
- lifestyle;
- community;
- prestige.

---

## 5.32 Second-Order Beneficiary Requires Transmission

Nearby improvement does not automatically re-rate old stock.

Need:

`Utility Transmission`
or
`Buyer-Reference Transmission`

Proximity alone is not enough.

---

## 5.33 Aggregate Demand != Unified Buyer Pool

`Aggregate Area Demand = Σ Project-Specific Buyer Pools`

Area liquidity is a demand reservoir.

It does not prove project interchangeability.

---

## 5.34 Usage Demand != Ownership Premium

Strong:
- occupancy;
- rent;
- commuting;
- tenant demand;

does not automatically create high capital pricing.

Possible:

`High Utility + Strong Rent + Weak Ownership Aspiration`

# 8. Additional SOP Diagnostics Validated in Prior Phases

## 8.1 Transaction Motive / Demand Authenticity Audit

Before using transaction count as proof of buyer depth, classify:

Own stay.

### Organic Investment Demand
Investor buys because repeatable tenant / usage demand exists.

Examples:
- student demand;
- employment-linked rentals;

Driven by financing mechanics.

- valuation mark-up;

`Transaction Count -> Transaction Motive -> Buyer-Depth Interpretation`

# Reusable execution safeguards

These implement existing principles without adding market-causal modules or G9 outputs.

1. Resolve exact developer, location, lifecycle, tenure, building form and residential parcel before price comparison. A scheme match is not a unit match.
2. Separate search-index access from full-source review, event date from crawl date, and an advertisement from verified availability/consideration. Deduplicate source lineage and transaction rows.
3. Normalise the housing task: actual area basis, original bedroom versus study/partition, legal parking, condition and incentives. Preserve excluded comps and evidence for adjustments. No mechanical unsupported adjustment percentages.
4. Attribute operations to the correct MC/JMB, manager, operator, landlord or contractor. Match tower, date, incident scope, financial position and remediation acceptance. Historic awards, old complaints and undated works do not establish current health/failure.
5. Verify whether a catalyst operates and whether its utility reaches the selected unit and buyer. Proximity widgets do not establish current access, opening hours, crossing friction or noise.
6. Preserve gate stops. Conditional downstream investigation may continue to understand the asset or identify an independent veto, but does not clear upstream unknowns. Unknown is not proof of structural failure.
7. Distinguish financial survival from adequate return. Reconcile entry cost, refurbishment, rent shortfall, recurring costs, interest, amortisation, disposal and applicable taxes; count principal once. Disclose excluded costs and opportunity cost. Cash reserve adequacy is a separate question.
8. Keep research completion separate from outcome coverage and realised accuracy. Never force Deploy or Reject to fill a test quota. Record prospective expectations, revisions and false negatives without retroactive rationalisation.
9. For auctions, distinguish reserve, sold price, availability and effective all-in basis. Separately allocate inherited liabilities, possession/repair costs, carry and future levies using actual sale conditions; an agent warning is not a legal debt determination.
10. Separate overall product positioning, a layout's family capability, actual buyer adoption and demonstrated willingness-to-pay. Check circulation, stairs, wet areas and parking. More nominal rooms/area need not produce more household utility.
11. Show incremental acquisition cash and monthly cost against a realistic alternative. Resolve header/body conflicts and voluntary-sale versus auction mechanisms before declaring a switch price.
12. A high-rise investment scope does not restrict the future buyer's substitute universe to high-rise. Test relevant landed alternatives at entry and required exit budgets, including all-in repairs, finance, possession, access, security and upkeep. Use them as diagnostic competitors without pooling incompatible values or assuming buyer transfer.
13. Separate completion age, physical condition, management finances and remaining title term. Newer does not automatically mean better governance, lower capex or a longer remaining lease.

## Evidence still required when relevant

- Field records: lift/access/noise at relevant hours, dated observations, actual plans, bays and permanent exposures.
- Governance records: collection, arrears, audited reserves, committed capex, insurance, enforcement and service continuity.
- Price records: effective consideration, incentives, lender valuation, motive and buyer/seller relationship. Financing-oriented advertising alone is not proof of manipulation.
- Case audit: preserve original call, cutoff, evidence, corrected premise, changed gate/rank/decision and then-knowable information. Hypothesis forms are not completed field checks or validated outcomes.

## Preservation versus compounding

Defensive mispricing and compounding quality are distinct. A defensible low basis can support downside protection; it does not prove cost-inclusive capital preservation or recurring appreciation. Compounding requires repeated future buyer willingness to pay higher capital quantum. Assess owner-compatible product, durable buyer depth, management/resident quality, controlled effective supply and existing demand separately; no feature bundle guarantees a winner.
