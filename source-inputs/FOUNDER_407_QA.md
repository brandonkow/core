## Stage 1 - Property Selection

124 questions, answered 2026-06-16 to 2026-06-17. Rules written into `docs/MY_INVESTMENT_FRAMEWORK.md`.

### A. Your Selection Philosophy

#### S1-1. What property types do you personally prefer: condo, apartment, serviced apartment, landed, shop apartment, commercial, industrial, land?

condo/serviced apartment.

#### S1-2. Which property types do you refuse to touch?

as a retail investor, not preferable to go into other type of property other than residential. In residential, I refuse to touch high rise developments more than 10 years, as well as flat and old apartments (unless use for cash out purpose).

#### S1-3. What does “best quality property” mean to you in practical terms?

Good build quality, mid size developers with good track record and striving to establish reputation (big ones don't mean always have good products), well-behaved residents and lastly responsible management team. Overall, it must able to serve own stay buyers and also investors (as in rentable).

#### S1-4. What makes a property “investment-grade but also own-stay friendly”?

I explained above.

#### S1-5. What is your minimum acceptable holding period?

3-5 years. Max 7 years

#### S1-6. Do you prefer capital appreciation, rental resilience, resale liquidity, or refinancing potential as the main priority?

Depend on preference. I cannot decide for other users as EstateLab ultimately is not only for my own use. I will plan to publish it as a professional AI real estate consultant.

#### S1-7. What is your biggest lesson from observing Malaysian property investors so far?

Each area has distinct market characteristics and culture, thus affecting investor preference and purchase decision. Value appreciation is stagnant but rental demand is still healthy, so investors are mainly focusing on holding the property long term and ensure healthy monthly cash flow from rental properties. However, most of them are looking at just the numbers without considering other aspects of the property. I have a theory. When they are just focusing on data, when market turns bad, every property becomes undervalued and cheap. Then how they can decide which one is temporarily cheap (will eventually increase in value back to norm), while some properties will only became cheaper and the market value will drop indefinitely. I would conclude that in a good market condition, every investment decision seem easy and effortless. Most of the investors don't really see through the worst condition and the intrinsic value of the property itself. This is why I want to perfect my second brain to not only remind me the framework at certain times when I got greedy, but also open for some people that really need the help.

### C. Price Segment

#### S1-17. What price ranges do you consider “right segment” in your target areas?

For now I am following my own financial profile, which is 400k to 500k range. This greatly depends on personal condition and belief. Some people think 500k range is the hardest to dispose. I can only say different price range of property will have its own target buyer. Just remember to not overpay for property that exceed the market value/beyond the segmentation of a particular area.

#### S1-18. How do you know a price segment has enough future buyers?

Based on median income. The salary of fresh graduate currently falls between 3.5k to 4.5k. Accounting 1/3 of their net salary for housing loan instalments, they can only afford somewhere around 300k, which is the price of an affordable housing provided by government. The reason I aim for 400k and above is to filter out the lower end residents while ensure my income can afford to pay the instalments (although I am renting out the unit).

#### S1-19. What price level becomes too expensive for the mass buyer pool?

550k and above.

#### S1-20. What price level becomes too cheap and signals poor quality?

250k and below. Many white collars still buy affordable house even though they can afford higher-priced property, therefore the demographic of the affordable house is still mixed and not too bad. But in terms of build quality, affordable house won't be good at all.

#### S1-21. Do you want to buy below median, near median, or premium within the area?

Depending on the area. If it's conventional local-focused area, I will buy below median. If it's high end area, I can opt to pay a price premium for certain high demand unit layouts.

#### S1-22. How much discount to market value is attractive enough?

20%  minimum, also depend on property type and area.

#### S1-23. How much premium would you pay for superior quality?

10% minimum

#### S1-24. What is the maximum all-in cost after renovation and transaction costs?

Reno should be only 5% of purchase price. I won't factor in transaction cost as that is mandatory. If you insist, I would say around 25% of cash outlay required and must allocated for cost. In the case of marked up financing, these costs are not self absorbed.
