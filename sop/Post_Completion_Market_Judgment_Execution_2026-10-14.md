## 1. Scope project appreciation and post-completion stewardship

Distinguish a project's comparable price trajectory from launch from an individual investor's cost-inclusive return. For a project that has already passed initial product/design screening, examine resident behaviour and management effectiveness as priority project-level explanations during approximately years 5-10 after completion. This is an analytical observation window, not a required holding period or proven universal two-factor law.

Keep the initial screening record. A later change in condition, use or a previously missed design defect can reopen it. A project-level diagnostic may isolate these factors for investigation; full valuation and investment decisions still consider competing supply, market/credit conditions and entry/exit terms. Normalise effective launch incentives, unit mix, condition and resale evidence before attributing an apparent price trend.

Record operating load, turnover, noise, rule compliance, wear, repair frequency, response/completion times, collections, reserves and funded works. Resident quality refers to observable conduct and operating effects, not intrinsic nationality, occupation or income.

The user's preferred outcome is to maintain the existing management charge while sustaining service and adequate funding, not to reduce the charge. Test whether lower use intensity reduces repair frequency and pressure for an increase. Separate fixed costs, variable wear, ageing and reserves. Unchanged fees alone do not demonstrate efficiency; underfunding, deferred works or a later special assessment can defeat that interpretation. No constant-fee forecast or veto on necessary increases is authorised.

Routing: Core 5.2, 5.15, 5.16, 5.25; G3/G4/G6/G7.

## 2. Separate resident, payer and accommodation decision-maker

Record who resides, who pays, who controls the budget, the budget ceiling, tenancy duration and renewal conditions. Third-party funding does not imply unlimited budget. Distinguish employer/family-funded demand from self-funded demand only with evidence.

For students, test institution-specific residence rules, year of study, freedom to live outside campus, provider packages and privacy alternatives. Private/public institution and nationality are not deterministic whole-unit/room choices. For staff, including shift and transport workers, test actual schedule, journey and ordinary lease fit. Presence of a school, hospital or airport is not project capture.

Routing: Core 5.26, 5.33, 5.35; G1/G2/G6.

## 3. Distinguish local attachment, ownership adoption and price support

Record temporary accommodation need, intention to remain locally, willingness to own, preference for the selected housing form/project, and funded ability to buy at the proposed exit price as separate fields. Include community ties and actual school/work routines.

Durable household appeal may support demand and saleability without supporting a higher next sale price. Do not convert tenant demand or a qualitative support thesis into a guaranteed monetary price floor. Test actual buyers, budgets, substitutes and downside clearing conditions. Investor-only exits remain eligible.

Routing: Core 5.4, 5.5, 5.27, 5.34; G2/G5/G6.

## 4. Match parking and security to the household task

Prioritise two legal, practically usable, reliably available bays for the city couple/family owner cohort that needs two vehicles. Check bay rights, independence, dimensions, access and total acquisition/recurring costs. Do not impose two bays as a universal gate on studios, single tenants or investor-led exits.

Compare high-rise and landed alternatives on matched total budget, condition, security, maintenance burden, access and daily routines. A dated belief about older neighbourhood stagnation is an attributed hypothesis; it cannot exclude all older landed comparables or manufacture a condo appreciation forecast.

Routing: Core 5.1, 5.15, 5.26; G2/G4/G5/G6.

## 5. Assign each claimed advantage to an observable benefit

Identify the primary proposed effect: rent level, vacancy duration, renewal, replacement demand, selling time, negotiation discount, buyer depth or sale price. Do not automatically book multiple benefits from one preference. Keep stronger liquidity separate from higher valuation and appreciation.

Require matched evidence for each numerical change. High price or prestige is not proof of quality or attractive investment terms. Assess convenience benefits and nuisance costs separately. Preserve no-premium/no-growth alternatives and actual negative cashflow.

Routing: Core 5.3, 5.23, 5.30, 5.34; G0/G1/G5/G6/G8.

## 6. Identify township demand by stage and origin

Test a possible sequence: early households displaced from older or expensive established neighbourhoods, followed by later arrivals drawn by employment, schools and a more mature ecosystem. Record source area, move motive, timing, budget, renting/buying and selected housing form. The sequence is contextual, not inevitable.

Do not count the same households as both spillover and additional employment demand. Do not transfer landed adoption into apartment adoption without evidence. Test whether different product lifestyles reduce direct competition while still allowing overlap through discounts, fit-out, upgrading and total budgets.

Routing: Core 5.6, 5.8, 5.27, 5.32, 5.35; G1/G2/G3/G6.

## 7. Investigate recoverable stigma and its exit

Record cause, repairability, responsible actor, observable recovery, catalyst transmission, review window, funded delay/no-improvement case and future buyer. Separate forced-sale or reputation effects from physical, governance, rights, financing and durable demand impairment.

User-verified market observations must be attributed to the user; do not falsely claim independent analyst verification. Transaction particulars require evidence before they become numerical inputs. Auction absorption does not prove profitable resale, and a discount to a reference is not a promised exit price. Absence of a structural building defect does not clear the other gates.

Routing: Core lifecycle, 5.24, 5.28, 5.30, 5.32; G0/G1/G3/G5/G6/G7.

## 8. Fit improvements to the user task and test recovery

Identify the tenant task, proposed improvement, incremental cost, useful life, maintenance/replacement burden, expected rental/retention effect and separately supported resale recovery. Compare a lower-cost alternative and a no-premium outcome.

Functional essentials may precede decorative preferences for a defined cohort. Fixed equipment or built-ins are not automatically recoverable value; movable items are not automatically worthless. Avoid a universal student aesthetic or furnishing specification.

Routing: Core 5.1, 5.15, 5.29; G3/G4/G6/G7.
