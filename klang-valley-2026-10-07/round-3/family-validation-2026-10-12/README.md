## Decision

**Keep Pines first for family-adoption verification, while retaining East Lake and Subang Avenue as investment challengers. All three remain G0 STOP / G9 Defer.** The public evidence strengthens context and exposes package differences. It does not establish family occupancy, renewal, owner-buyer depth or a numerical management premium.

The user's judgment remains a plausible account of tenant choice, not a claim that every household behaves alike. It cannot establish an investment winner independently of price and costs. The earlier inference from large units to demonstrated family demand remains withdrawn; this packet does not restore it.

## What retention and refurbishment must deliver

One extra paid month adds 3,800/2,500/2,000 annual cash to the original Pines/Subang/East Lake scenarios respectively. This is where a real household-retention advantage could matter. No such advantage is measured or entered into the bases.

For Subang's advertised terms, a RM100 monthly concession over 24 paid months costs RM2,400. Avoiding one otherwise lost RM2,400 month offsets that concession before reletting fees, defaults or other costs. A two-year contract does not guarantee 24 paid months. The scenario engine still assumes eleven paid months annually unless explicitly labelled otherwise.

An additional RM30k refurbishment requires roughly **RM545 extra monthly rent across 55 collected months** merely to recover that extra outlay over five years, before incremental expenses, financing/time value and any return target. It is not enough to point to an attractive renovated rental photograph or a higher ask.

At Pines' original rent, an extra 30k refurbishment raises initial cash from 145k to 175k and nominal break-even sale from about 752k to 783k. An extra 100/month recurring fee removes 1,200 annual cash in every case. Good management may preserve desirability while still requiring real spending; its economic value cannot be inferred by setting repair costs to zero.

Five-year hold; 5% acquisition and 3% disposal allowances; inherited refurbishment. 8% equity return is illustrative, not user-approved.

These are required-sale burdens, not forecasts or market values. Even Pines at 700k/4,500 still loses roughly RM12,157 at a five-year unchanged-price exit under this cost model. Positive annual carry does not establish a satisfactory total investment return.

Investor-led resale remains eligible for all three. Owner-family adoption, where actually demonstrated, may broaden the exit universe, but a family tenant is not automatically a funded family buyer. At unchanged base rents, 6% gross-yield capitalisation gives 760k for Pines, 500k for Subang and 400k for East Lake; 7% gives about 651k/429k/343k. These illustrative investor prices are sensitive to the required yield and are not observed clearing prices. No family-premium yield compression is assigned.

The results include rate 5.5%, rent down 20%, additional 30k works, reduced bank valuation, 25-year actual term, 12-month exit delay and a combined six-paid-month/works/rate shock. Standard comparison remains 90%/4%/35. Reduced borrowing can improve carry while increasing equity at risk; it is not better property performance. Synthetic reserve outputs are not the user's balance sheet or position sizing.

No validated input was promoted, so the historical integrated portfolio release remains intact. Actual allocation requires admitted units and updated joint concentration/duration funding tests, not extrapolation from these single-case sensitivities.
