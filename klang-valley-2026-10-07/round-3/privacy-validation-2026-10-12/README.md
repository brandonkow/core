## Decision

**Keep Eve first for focused research, with Maxim as a live challenger. Neither is admitted for investment: G0 STOP / G9 Defer.** This public pass establishes concrete asking alternatives and the sensitivity of the preference; it does not validate a paid privacy premium, occupancy advantage or financeable exit.

The strongest incremental lead is a one-bay 680-sf Eve advertised at RM370,000, below the unchanged RM390,000 historical scenario. Its advertised package fits the compact expression sufficiently to investigate, but availability, condition and effective consideration remain unverified. It is an entry-price lead, not proof of market mispricing. [ES2](https://www.propertyguru.com.my/property-listing/eve-suite-nzx-square-for-sale-by-tom-ting-502001977)

At the original prices and equal RM2,000 rent, annual cash differs by only RM1.80. A preference based on accommodation demand can guide evidence effort, but cannot carry a numerical premium without matched collections, costs and exit support. The RM370k lead improves the case even with **zero assumed privacy premium**.

The monthly column is **rent minus instalment minus combined service/sinking proxy**; actual management-only coverage is unverified. Annual cash includes eleven paid months and the inherited monthly other-cost allowance: Eve fee 280 + other 180; Maxim fee 300 + other 200. These are assumptions, not invoices.

The RM2,200 rents and RM410k price are sensitivities, not authenticated terms. Rent evidence is asking-only throughout. Eve at RM390k/RM2,200 is approximately annual-neutral, with essentially no contingency. Eve at RM370k/RM2,200 has about RM987 annual headroom; one extra vacant month or a modest repair removes it. At RM2,000 rent, the annual-neutral price under these costs is approximately **RM344,628 for Eve and RM334,590 for Maxim**. At the original prices, annual-neutral rent is about **RM2,197 for either**. These are conditional cash thresholds, not fair values, mandatory rejection rules or recommended offer ceilings. Missing taxes, transaction-specific costs and actual operating requirements may tighten them.

The original base annual deficits are RM2,169.67 and RM2,171.48. Eve's RM40 lower assumed monthly fee/other cost almost exactly offsets its RM10k higher price. That assumed cost advantage is not verified operational superiority.

## How much advantage would justify a price difference?

Let k = 0.0039849726693, the monthly instalment per RM of price under 90%/4%/35. With eleven paid months each, annual-cash parity requires:

`Eve price - Maxim price = [11 × (Eve rent - Maxim rent) / 12 - ((Eve fee + Eve other) - (Maxim fee + Maxim other))] / k`

Here the final cost term means `(Eve fee + Eve other) - (Maxim fee + Maxim other)`, currently -RM40/month. For clarity, no fee is subtracted twice.

| Sustained monthly rent advantage for Eve | Extra price consistent with annual-cash parity, at the assumed cost difference |
|---|---:|
| 0 | RM10,038 |
| 100 | RM33,041 |
| 200 | RM56,044 |

These limits are **cash parity only**. They do not support resale value, initial equity return or an actual premium. Each additional RM100 of supported monthly rent contributes RM1,100 annually at eleven paid months; it can be defeated by furnishing costs, bundled services, concessions or turnover.

Concrete reversals:

- Eve at RM2,100 with only ten paid months produces about **-RM3,170/year**, worse than Maxim's original -RM2,171. Higher advertised rent can coexist with poorer collected income.
- One additional collected month each year is worth RM2,000 of annual cash at RM2,000 rent. However, even twelve paid months leave Eve's original model about **-RM170/year**. We have no evidence to substitute twelve for eleven.
- An additional RM100/month fee removes RM1,200/year. A one-off extra RM10k refurbishment would take Eve's five-year flat-price loss from approximately RM31.6k to RM41.6k, worse than Maxim's RM36.4k.
- The 27-point rent/price grid produces both winners. It is a sensitivity grid without probability weights, not a frequency estimate of which project wins.

## Exit and finance remain binding

Five-year hold; 5% acquisition allowance, 3% disposal allowance, Eve refurbishment 15k and Maxim 20k. All are inherited, pre-tax illustrations; no legal/tax quote or full cost certification. The 8% equity hurdle is illustrative, not user-approved. Principal is counted once.

| Scenario | Initial cash | Year-5 debt | Nominal break-even sale | Sale for illustrative 8% equity return | Total profit if resold at entry price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Maxim historical base | 77,000 | 317,185 | 417,569 | 457,687 | -36,442 |
| Eve historical base | 73,500 | 325,532 | 422,557 | 460,979 | -31,580 |
| Eve lower asking-price lead | 70,500 | 308,838 | 397,324 | 433,231 | -26,504 |

Required sales are burdens, not forecasts. At unchanged RM2,000 monthly rent, gross-yield capitalisation gives RM400k at 6%, RM342,857 at 7% and RM300k at 8%. These are investor sensitivities, not observed exit yields or clearing values. RM400k remains below both original scenarios' nominal break-even sales. The lower-price Eve scenario needs about RM397k at exit merely to break even nominally, still above its RM370k entry.

An investor-led exit is eligible. It requires repeatable net collections and a buyer who can fund that price; an owner-occupier exit is not mandatory. Household tenancy, household purchase willingness and borrower approval remain different evidence questions.

Actual-finance sensitivities retain the common 90% comparison. At a bank value 15% below purchase price, financing 90% of that lower value raises entry cash to approximately RM126,150 for Eve and RM128,300 for Maxim. Better monthly carry from borrowing less is not an asset improvement. At 5.5% rates, annual cash deteriorates to about -RM6,139 and -RM6,039 respectively.

The combined twelve-month shock adds RM30k works, six paid months and rent down 20% at 5.5%. Cash burn after entry is about RM48.5k for each. Synthetic reserves in the frozen model are not the user's balance sheet or an allocation recommendation. A 72-month hold is separately calculated: its lower nominal break-even reflects this particular amortisation/rent path and does not establish a lower risk or better time-adjusted return.
