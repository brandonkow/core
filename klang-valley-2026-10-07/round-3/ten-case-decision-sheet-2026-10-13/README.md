# Ten-case evidence priorities and decision boundaries

Completed 13 October 2026. Synthesis baseline: `2e4a1c9d7d1c155456f9601fd217baa4044a075b`. Uses the four public packets dated 12-13 October, with unchanged historical inputs; no fresh market observations, new case IDs or regional coverage.

**Keep all ten cases at G0 STOP / G9 Defer.** Nine historical scenarios cover the simplified monthly standard; only Parkview and Arc have positive annual cash after the assumed vacancy and operating allowances. All ten lose money if sold after five years at their own entry prices. These findings distinguish levels of underwriting; they are not evidence that the properties are universally unattractive.

Eve stays first for privacy evidence, Pines for family-adoption evidence and Zeva for captured-demand-diversity evidence. These are three different questions, not a global buy ranking. Maxim, East Lake, Subang Avenue and Solstice remain live challengers.

## Read the numbers consistently

RM throughout. Standard loan: 90% of purchase price, 4%, 35 years. Monthly balance = rent minus instalment minus the combined service/sinking proxy; a verified management-only charge is still missing. Annual cash = eleven paid months of rent minus twelve months of instalments, fees and other allowances.

No rent growth, privacy premium, family premium, diversity premium or catalyst uplift is booked. Price, rent and recurring charges remain unvalidated assumptions. Pre-tax calculations include inherited refurbishment, 5% acquisition and 3% disposal allowances; transaction-specific charges, taxes and unquoted works remain missing. Unknown costs are not certified zero.

A rounded cash-neutral threshold is a sensitivity, not a fair value, offer ceiling, margin of safety or permission to bypass G0. It changes when any other input changes. Current simplified monthly shortfall retains lowest research priority, rather than permanent Reject. Annual-negative cases are shown separately without silently redefining that user rule.

The sale uplift is a five-year cumulative **required burden**, not expected appreciation. Annual-neutral carry does not recover acquisition/disposal costs or refurbishment. Even a verified rental surplus would still require a defensible exit buyer, financing, valuation and funded downside. Conversely, a flat-price loss alone does not prove a structurally bad asset: a price-recovery thesis must have an identifiable, falsifiable mechanism and cannot be assumed to close the gap.

A further RM30k initial refurbishment adds RM30k cash and RM30k flat-exit loss; it does not change recurring annual cash. This separation is especially relevant to original-condition Pines and Subang. Strong management may protect demand while still requiring paid capital works.

## 5. Interpretation control, misses and next action

The three market-judgment hypotheses remain Unresolved. PAL-D01 remains an Unresolved Evidence-Judgment Divergence Flag. This synthesis adds no new flag: monthly coverage versus annual cash, or annual cash versus total return, are different measures rather than contradictory evidence about the same proposition. No intuition independently authorises investment.

The audit traps exposed here are: treating near-neutral carry as conviction; pricing a qualitative preference before measurement; ranking by the best held branch; cancelling repair exposure because buildings are similarly old; and claiming buyer depth from asking rents or neighbourhood context.
