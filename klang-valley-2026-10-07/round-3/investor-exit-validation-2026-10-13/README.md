## Decision

**Keep Zeva first for investigating captured demand diversity; retain Solstice as the lower-capital investment challenger. Neither is an established investment winner. Both remain G0 STOP / G9 Defer.**

Solstice has a documented university accommodation channel and visible non-student marketing/employment context. These observations justify investigating replacement demand, not declaring student exclusivity disproved or diverse actual occupancy established. Zeva's retail/rail context remains plausible; its selected compact's tenant mix and resale depth are still unmeasured. Neither receives an automatic diversity premium.

At the historical inputs, Solstice needs RM6,500 less initial cash and has about RM478 less annual cash shortfall. Zeva needs only about RM43 more monthly rent across eleven paid months, or 0.40 more paid months per year at RM1,200, to match annual carry. **That is not enough by itself to recover extra upfront capital or equalise total return.** The exit-price burden is the more consequential test.

The 8% hurdle is inherited illustration, not a user-approved target. Required prices are burdens, not predictions. Zeva requires roughly **RM17,771 more sale consideration to break even** under these inputs. Its better-demand hypothesis must explain why actual funded buyers can absorb the relevant price, not merely why the neighbourhood is more mixed.

At unchanged rent, the same gross investor yield gives the same conditional capitalisation for either unit:

| Hypothetical gross yield required by buyer | Price | Buyer annual carry at 90% / 4% / 35y and inherited costs |
|---|---:|---:|
| 6% | 240,000 | -2,237 |
| 7% | 205,714 | -597 |
| 8% | 180,000 | 632 |

These are not observed required yields or equal fair values. More stable collections could affect price, but no yield compression is assigned. At 240k, Zeva's seller would make only about RM2,003 nominal profit after five years, while the illustrated next buyer still carries an annual deficit. A seller's break-even price is not automatically an attractive buyer's entry. Current negative carry does not permanently exclude a funded buyer or evidenced transition thesis; the source of future improvement and the cost of waiting still need proof.

The separate net-income illustration uses annual NOI of 9,240 (eleven collected rents minus fee/other allowances, before debt). Capitalising that at 5% or 6% gives 184,800 or 154,000; do not compare those as if they were gross-yield quotations. Future-buyer payments use 90% in both the 4%/35-year standard and 5.5%/25-year credit stress. No bank valuation, approval, buyer equity or resale velocity has been authenticated.

Rate 5.5%, rent down 20%, higher fees, 30k works, 15% lower bank valuation, 25-year term, delayed exit and missing-cost buffers are included. Lower lending can improve carry while requiring more cash; it is not better asset performance. Joint 12-/24-month shocks assume correlated pressure, not diversification inferred from different postcodes. The synthetic 500k cash / 150k reserve survives those two particular tests, but that neither sizes a real position nor replaces the preserved programme-level stress work. A longer hold can reduce nominal break-even through principal repayment in this model; it still ties up capital and is not proof of an easier exit.
