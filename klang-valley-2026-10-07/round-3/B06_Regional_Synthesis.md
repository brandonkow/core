## Regional decision

There is no defensible single executable winner. The comparatively attractive income expressions are small-ticket mature stock, but price, parcel, lease and operating-cost evidence is weakest precisely where apparent yields improve. Urban 360 compact remains the first **price-and-lease verification** lead, not a purchase recommendation. Its family version tests whether more household utility compensates for higher absolute service charges. PV 21 and the altered 168 Park compact remain useful opposing controls; neither becomes investable because a spreadsheet shows 6% gross.

The new-launch Kwasa story belongs to a different timing problem. Operational regional rail, proposed town-centre amenities and a published starting price cannot establish rent at handover, actual progressive financing or resale buyer depth. Tujuh and D'Evia receive threshold and failure-path analysis; no invented current rental income is used.

Every current-shortfall expression stays in the lowest research-priority tier. A future rental transition may reopen it if achieved evidence, transmission, timing and funded carry are demonstrated. This is not a permanent structural rejection.

## A41 — Setapak, Wangsa Maju and Danau Kota

**Boundary and household tasks.** These are connected but non-identical markets. Wangsa Metroview tests a mature family apartment associated with Wangsa Maju access; PV 21 tests a 900-sf two-bedroom in the Danau Kota/Platinum catchment; 222 Residency tests an off-Jalan Gombak household location; Zen Suites tests mall-integrated studio/SOHO living. A road called Jalan Gombak does not move 222 Residency into the Sri Gombak cohort. The Ridge is counted once in A43 and cross-linked here.

Students, local workers, car-based households and rail users are separate demand hypotheses. University or mall proximity does not establish a paying tenant share. Treat route reliability, station entry, road crossings, parking and school routines as potentially binding preferences; no advertised drive time is an audited peak-hour trip. In particular, a Jalan Langkawi MRT widget is not present rail service. The official MRT 3 source in packet 01 is an approval/land-acquisition milestone.

**Product and substitute choice.** R330 gives more internal family space at a higher total ticket than the compact R337; extra bedrooms do not automatically earn extra rent. R337 is a real advertised whole-unit studio expression, but the SOHO description requires approved-use and lending checks. No partition-room income or Airbnb receipts are included. PV 22 future compact/family supply and M Astra/M Azura family cohorts can compete for the same budgets even though their specifications and delivery stages differ. Larger PV-family or townhouse stock is a functional alternative, not an interchangeable PSF comparable.

**Price discovery and counter-thesis.** A vendor record reports a 1152-sf Metroview sale at 500 k while the retained 1150-sf ask is 350 k. This could be seller-specific distress; equally, the record may reflect a different condition, consideration, unit or form. Similar 499–500 k records and geocoding inconsistencies make a 30% discount assertion premature. The separately found Metroview townhouse series is excluded. Neither pooled historical medians nor repeated portal ads repair G0. Sources F29–F30 in [the source ledger](B06_Evidence_Register.json).

**Decision and exit.** Retain PV 21 as a low-ticket income hypothesis but resolve its sale/rent wording conflict first. Zen is component-held; Metroview and 222 Residency are current-shortfall studies. Test actual family and investor willingness to pay, not an assumed owner-occupier premium. A lower funded entry, credible higher whole-unit lease or lower verified fee could reverse the order. New supply absorption that requires incentives would weaken the rent-uplift thesis even if primary developer sales were strong. Next finite evidence: matched unit offers and two independent comparable leases, approved layouts, current invoices/MC accounts and a documented station/household route.

## A42 — Gombak / Sri Gombak

**Boundary.** Urban 360 is the Sri Gombak local-worker/household experiment; it is not a stand-in for all properties carrying Gombak in an address. Regional LRT/ECRL access must be decomposed into first-mile travel, transfers and timing. No direct-walk rail premium is credited from a project label.

**Compact versus family.** The inherited 511-sf studio at 250 k/1450 has much stronger monthly coverage than the new 1001-sf 350 k/1800 family expression. The family sale advertises RM 375.23 management, an occupied basic unit and two bays; it is not a ready furnished 1800 lease. [Sale source](https://www.mudah.my/for-sale-urban-360-taman-sri-gombak-gombak-selangor-115844924.htm). A costed furnished 1900 branch still has negative annual carry. Larger triplex/garden stock exists in the portal profile but serves a different budget and maintenance task; the observed 750 k 1459-sf listing does not justify applying compact yields.

**Counterexample and reference discipline.** New 220 k studio headers might transform income economics. One exact ad instead says 270 k in its body; another offers generic finance promotions without net-price terms. These are conflicting price hypotheses, not proof that 220 k is fraudulent or that 270 k is true. The historical transaction sample mixes roughly 506-sf and family sizes and ends in 2025. A vendor's Sold auction row has no accessible price/parcel proof. None is a valid current 511-sf clearing band.

**Exit, opportunity cost and next evidence.** A single worker/couple and a local family may both be viable exit buyers; small quantum helps only if banks accept the parcel and buyers accept its condition/fees. Studio demand can weaken when a modest extra budget rents a family unit; family value can weaken when local landed accommodation better serves parking and privacy. Obtain the exact 220/250/270 k offer reconciliation and lease before any governance or upgrade spending. The family case is a control with only a thin monthly buffer; the studio loses standard coverage at the independently surfaced 1200 lower-rent scenario. No future transport catalyst rescues base rent automatically.

## A43 — Melawati, KL East and Ukay

**Boundary and tasks.** The Ridge addresses smaller households seeking a mall/amenity setting; Saville addresses more conventional family space; Spring Ville addresses budget-constrained Ukay households. These cannot share one postcode premium or commute assumption. Schools, drop-off, vehicle storage, circulation, lift availability and exact hillside/road exposure determine which buyers can substitute between them. No parcel-level slope, flood or noise defect has been established by this desk review.

**Budget and lifecycle contrasts.** R331 compact and R227 family each use 450 k but different rent/space/cost hypotheses. The Ridge 2400 furnished branch includes more works and delayed collection. It does not prove superior owner-occupier desirability. Spring Ville 250 k is a low-quantum three-bedroom counterexample, but the specific sale says Bumi and the project profile contradicts the unit's tenure. Lower 220 k marketing and furnished 1500 rent are carried as separate, costed branches. Eligibility is a property/transaction condition, never inferred from the user's name or identity.

The Reya's larger 1350–2210-sf cohort is a forward family-upgrade competitor, not an equivalent 651-sf compact comp. Its developer preview take-up does not measure occupied demand. The same applies to landed KL East alternatives: stronger family utility can cap condo willingness to pay without requiring that every household prefer landed.

**Decision.** No universal compact or family winner emerges. The Ridge and Saville only barely meet the simplified current monthly test at base; Spring's lower price may improve affordability but restrictions, maintenance and ageing common property can consume the benefit. All three base annual carries are negative. A favourable 1500 furnished Spring outcome becomes slightly annual-positive after the additional fit-out, while its five-year flat-price result remains negative. That is a useful counterexample to absolute rejection, not a validated thesis.

**Exit and finite reopening.** Verify Spring's legal buyer universe and current charges, then original plan, governance, exposure and actual whole-unit rent. For The Ridge, verify permitted 1+1 layout, actual furnishing/possession date and comparable tenant turnover. An apparently attractive view or mall interface can be offset by noise, privacy, lifts or common-area costs; these observations require site and MC evidence before scoring. ECRL/other transport timing stays a scenario until a measured door-to-door advantage changes achieved rents or resale bids.

## A44 — Batu Caves and Selayang

**Boundary.** Amara is the retained Batu Caves/Raintree family control; Selayang Point tests mature family accommodation associated with the hospital/commercial area; 168 Park tests a newer/revived development and smaller quantum. A Batu Caves mailing address on 168 Park does not make it equivalent to Amara's station-side household task. **Selection and lifecycle.** R336 family 335 k/1500 is more directly comparable to ordinary long-term household use than room-ad aggregates. R335 compact 265 k/1500 looks stronger financially but discloses an owner-added partition. It cannot represent verified original two-bedroom supply. A separate 624-sf ad says one room; the government-hosted brochure has mixed phase/footnote information. Current approved layout, block, CCC, defect history and charges must be identified before the newer-product preference is accepted.

The primary auction listing for a 560-sf Block A unit is a separate 260 k reserve, not a transacted equivalent of the 624-sf voluntary sale. Government brochure text contains a multi-block programme; it does not establish that every unit is delivered or competes simultaneously. Older Amara and Selayang Point retain possible family utility but potential major-works/fee-reset burdens. Neither age nor revival history independently proves poor management.

**Decision, downside and exit.** All three base annual carries are negative. Amara and Selayang Point have standard shortfalls, hence lowest current-income research priority. 168 Park requires a layout/component hold despite a more attractive gross yield. Genuine original studio/one-bedroom stock remains eligible; RES 280 partition-room and low-rise DahliaB observations do not satisfy that category. A failed compact search is not proof there is no compact supply.

Reopen on the exact block's approved plan/CCC, current lease and bills, building accounts/works plan and matched voluntary transactions. Compare the needed exit quantum with both older family condos and the remaining new-phase inventory. Check road crossings, hospital access, local flooding/drainage records and insurability for the actual parcel; do not import an aggregator's area-level hazard/crime label into a building verdict.

**Two different clocks.** D'Sara provides a completed-stock rent/exit control near the existing rail corridor; Kwasa new launches require construction, amenities and absorption timing. An established station is a location asset, while the usable dwelling, first-mile route and local services still need proof. **Completed and distressed alternatives.** R 332410 k/1700 fails current simplified coverage. The funding illustration deliberately adds a first-year no-income period and works/exit costs; it is not an auction bid limit or legal advice.

**Kwasa cohort and supply.** Tujuh supplies an identifiable 550-sf 1 BR/573-unit primary product control; D'Evia supplies a 657-sf marketed 2 BR control, with larger family layouts advertised separately. Source versions and net packages remain unresolved. At their illustrative price floors, post-completion standard rent needs roughly 2219/2287 per month, 6% gross needs 2470/2491 and annual cash neutrality needs 2617/2691. These are requirements, not rent forecasts. See [the financial paper](B06_Financial_Underwriting.md).

Other announced Kwasa schemes include mixed landed/condo and affordable programmes. They are separated by form, rights, phase and launch/delivery stage in the supply paper; no double-counted township total or assumed scarcity supports an exit premium. A new job/retail ecosystem could improve rents, but it can also induce competing supply before enough tenants arrive.

**Decision and finite reopening.** Current D'Sara shortfall is lowest research priority; SOVO/auction expressions are held; new-launch controls Defer pending a matched net offer, approved component, current project evidence, draw schedule and credible take-up-to-occupancy transmission. Family, compact and investor buyers remain eligible, with actual lending/equity requirements tested at the exit quantum. Compare a delayed Kwasa purchase with carrying completed stock or holding cash; no assumed refinance or forced sale bridges the funding gap.

## What would change the regional ranking?

1. Verified Urban 360220 k effective entry plus an ordinary 1450 lease would make its conditional annual carry positive; the 270 k body scenario reverses that improvement. Even the favourable case loses money at a flat five-year exit under current allowances.
2. Urban 360 studio at 1200 can lose monthly coverage to its family control. The preferred layout depends on achievable leases and fees, not a permanent bedroom rule.
3. PV 21 and Zen can switch coverage order within the scenario grid; unresolved price versus unresolved SOHO rights requires different evidence, not a single confidence score.
4.168 Park versus Selayang Point can reverse when compact rent weakens and family rent improves. The cheaper compact's altered plan could narrow its exit universe before price becomes relevant.
5. The Ridge's assumed furnishing uplift is neither free nor immediate. Without it, a mall narrative does not create an income advantage; with it, current annual and terminal burdens must still be funded.
6. Kwasa catalysts require a rental cohort to form. More launch activity alone can make the forward case worse through competing supply.

The [financial results](B06_Financial_Results.json) include these assumption tests. All 91 synthetic two-asset combinations pass the stated reserve floor; nearby projects share demand, roads and refinancing exposure, so this is a funding result rather than diversification proof.
