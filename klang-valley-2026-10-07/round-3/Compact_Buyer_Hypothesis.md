# Compact-unit buyer and exit hypothesis

Date: 7 October 2026. Reusable execution treatment plus programme research hypothesis. Outside frozen Core.

## User judgment and evidentiary status

The Senior Market Red Team's judgment is that small-unit resale currently depends mainly on investors, while future furnished compact units may also attract younger owner-occupiers. This is informed market judgment. It is not a measured buyer-share statistic and cannot be asserted identically across all 61 catchments.

Base underwriting provisionally treats compact resale as investor-dependent unless project-specific evidence supports a broader buyer mix. It credits no speculative owner premium or assumed annual rent growth. This keeps studios and original one-bedroom units eligible without treating every compact advertisement as a residential parcel.

## Testable mechanism

Hypothesis H-C01: At a sufficiently accessible total acquisition and monthly ownership cost, an original residential compact unit with useful living space, workable parking/access, privacy and acceptable management can attract younger owner buyers seeking a furnished home. Lower immediate fit-out cost and daily convenience may improve adoption, but only if ownership is competitive with renting and with slightly larger or older substitutes.

Scope: project, approved unit type, furnishing condition, total price and buyer task. Aesthetic furnishing preference alone is not durable scarcity. Replaceable furniture depreciates; recurring replacement cost remains in underwriting.

Predictions: ordinary secondary transfers show buyers intending and actually using the unit as a principal residence; lending is available on the real parcel; owner willingness to pay persists beyond launch incentives; comparable ownership costs and local routines explain the choice; completed resales are not dependent on short-stay promises or a developer rental guarantee.

Falsifiers: investor resale remains the only evidenced channel at the tested price; young residents overwhelmingly choose to rent while purchasing larger/other homes; financeability or permitted use limits ownership; a modest extra budget buys materially better utility; furnishing premium disappears at resale; operating costs or turnover undermine privacy and owner appeal.

Absence of motive data is unresolved evidence, not proof of either adoption or rejection. Occupancy, young tenants, furnished asking prices, national homeownership and a ban on short stays do not independently establish young owner demand.

## Investigation design

- A - Data limitation: separate registered transfers from asks; age, occupancy and purchase motive are usually missing. Portal layout labels, incentive effects and new/secondary cohorts can distort apparent demand.
- B - Judgment bias: actively challenge both the optimistic future-owner story and the entrenched belief that compact products can only sell to investors. Do not award premium because the analyst likes the interior.
- C - Omitted variable: test original usable layout, ownership quantum, down payment, parking, privacy, access, maintenance and household duration against actual substitutes.
- D - Structural change: identify a dated employment, affordability, household or access change and show transmission to completed purchases. A broad generation narrative without conversion evidence is insufficient.

Search for contrary examples in both directions. A young tenant is not automatically a future buyer. An investor purchase does not prove absence of owner value.

## Financial exit tests

For a supported monthly whole-unit rent R and an assumed buyer gross-yield requirement y, the diagnostic investor price is 12R/y. This is a sensitivity, not fair value; it omits buyer-specific financing and must be checked against net costs and actual comparable clearing.

Moving a buyer requirement from 6% to 7%, with unchanged rent, lowers this diagnostic price by 14.29%. Moving from 6% to 8% lowers it by 25%. Furnishing or an optimistic owner story does not neutralise this repricing risk.

The buyer's ownership burden is the full amortising instalment plus management and other ownership costs, with deposit and purchase costs considered separately. Compare with renting the same utility and actual alternatives; do not equate gross household income with a young single buyer's income.

Sources for context only: [DOSM 2024 household income release](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/household-income-survey-report--malaysia--states-2024) and [DOSM 2024 basic amenities release](https://www.dosm.gov.my/portal-main/release-content/basic-amenities-survey-report-malaysia-2024). Their state/national aggregates do not establish age-by-layout owner demand. They provide no project-level price uplift.
