## D01 — Compact Maxim versus the inherited three-bedroom research preference

**⚠ DIVERGENCE FLAG**

**Affected scope:** KV01, 825-sqft two-bedroom, ordinary rental with small-household exit; compared with the inherited 1050-sqft Maxim and M Vertica cases. Five-year diagnostic horizon; current asks/rents at the cutoff.

**Observed Evidence:** E01/E02 show a lower purchase quantum and an ordinary furnished rent ask consistent with 6%-plus gross arithmetic. The earlier Cheras selection favoured usable three-bedroom expressions; it did not establish that a two-bedroom alternative was inferior.

**Contradicting Judgment / Observation:** The informed household-exit hypothesis favours more usable rooms and parking, but the lower-quantum two-bedroom could be accessible to more financeable buyers. Neither future depth nor superiority is observed.

| Explanation | Discriminating test and work performed | Result / residual burden |
|---|---|---|
| A. Data limitation | Obtained same-marketed-area sale/rent ads, lower RM1799 rent lead E62 and project transactions E03. Check furnishing, parking, original plan, effective price and duplicated ads. | Partial sale versus furnished rent and one bay remain important; achieved rent and matched clearing unknown. |
| B. Judgment bias | Explicitly reversed the initial room-count preference; compare both room counts using the same 90/4/35 rules and separate costs. | A two-bedroom research lead survives; prior preference is not proof of three-bedroom dominance. Genuine owner demand remains to be tested. |
| C. Omitted variable | Test second-room usability, one-bay constraints and household budget against the third-room alternative. | Test planned for original plans, same-weekend choices and buyer motives; public ads do not supply these. |
| D. Structural change | Seek evidence that smaller households/financing budgets now favour this type, rather than assuming an enduring demographic shift. | No longitudinal buyer-mix or credit evidence obtained. No structural-change credit. |

**Falsifiable Hypothesis:** At an actual clean price near RM380k, the original two-bedroom type can support ordinary collected rent and a financeable small-household resale, making it at least as worthy of investigation as the older RM480k three-bedroom expression.

**Evidence Required:** Same-package signed/renewed rent; itemised fees and fit-out; original plan/bay rights; matched ordinary resale and buyer motives for both layouts.

**Falsification Conditions:** Rent fails the modeled standard threshold after comparable furnishing; the second room or bay materially limits use; purchase rights/price differ; or the next buyer repeatedly substitutes a three-bedroom within the relevant budget. A small paper cash advantage alone does not confirm the thesis.

**Core Module Routing:** Asset Fit; Buyer Universe; Comparable Set; Exit Buyer; Financing Risk. G0/G2/G4/G5/G6/G7.

**Current Status:** **Partially Resolved** only as a sampling correction; superiority and investability unresolved. Keep KV01 Defer. Next active research pass must obtain discriminatory evidence before ranking above three-bedroom alternatives. No automatic rent-growth forecast.

**Contradicting Judgment / Observation:** Strong urban rental willingness-to-pay may coexist with owner resistance to a small unit at the required future total price. This is a forward-risk hypothesis, not proof of poor owner desirability.

| Explanation | Discriminating test and work performed | Result / residual burden |

| D. Structural change | Investigate actual absorption and competing phase delivery rather than treating historical rent asks as forward truth. | Relevant inherited Maluri supply work retained; no new quantified net-demand transmission established. |

**Falsification Conditions:** Genuine owner-led completed resales and sustained demand support the relevant quantum after costs, with no reliance on incentives or room/night revenue. Conversely, consistent preference for larger alternatives supports the risk hypothesis.

**Core Module Routing:** Owner-Occupier Desirability; Buyer Universe; Asset Fit; Exit Buyer; Supply Structure; Externality Ceiling. G2/G3/G4/G5/G6.

**Current Status:** **Unresolved**. Defer, lower research priority than more affordable standard-positive household leads. A mechanical RM640k price at 6% using RM3200 rent is not fair value or an available offer.

**Contradicting Judgment / Observation:** The 450-sqft expression offers limited growing-family utility and may depend more heavily on investors for resale. That informed product/buyer hypothesis challenges a yield-only ranking. It is not an observed absence of owner buyers.

| Explanation | Discriminating test and work performed | Result / residual burden |

| D. Structural change | Checked university alternatives E49, which identify other accommodation channels including Antara. | Evidence contradicts a captive-demand assumption, but does not quantify net tenant loss, enrolment, occupancy or structural decline. **Falsification Conditions:** Rent or paid months fail cash-collection assumptions, fees/works erase surplus, lender/usage restrictions narrow the buyer pool, or repeated comparable exits require discounts unsupported by net income.
