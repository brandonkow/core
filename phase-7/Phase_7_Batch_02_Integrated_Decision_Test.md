## P7-01 - Kiara Park high-rise continuation

### G0 - Reference-price validity

**Unresolved; no discount claim.** Retain the original block-168/level-five 1,470 sf, one-bay RM850k advertisement from Batch 01 as K1. The high-floor two-bay RM1.35m offer is a different expression (B2-K01). A third-floor walk-up with two tandem bays is excluded from this high-rise study (B2-K02). The same development name cannot overcome different housing form.

The auctioneer verifies a **past RM558k reserve** for 168-7-8 on 22 January 2026 (B2-K03); it does not verify a sale. A separate agent warning states outstanding amounts above RM200k (B2-K04). No formal debt statement or sale conditions were obtained. This information prevents calling the low reserve an immediately recoverable gap. No verified high-rise clearing range is supplied.

### G1 - Market mechanism

Interpretation retained from Batch 01: recurring TTDI daily-life utility can sustain an established family dwelling without a new catalyst. The high-rise expression must earn its own acceptance; courtyard reputation and landed wealth are not transferable evidence. Lifecycle: mature utility/preservation candidate. Price-recognition stage: unresolved for K1, with the two-bay ask imposing a substantially greater exit burden. No new market-growth forecast.

### G2 - Buyer universe

Primary hypothesised buyer: a household seeking TTDI high-rise living at a manageable total budget, willing to own ageing stock. Secondary: a landlord supported by repeatable household tenancy. False borrowed buyer: a landed loyalist or courtyard-preferring purchaser assumed to accept the tower automatically. One-bay access may narrow two-car family demand, but no hard two-bay universal rule follows.

The user's prior parking concern is retained as practitioner evidence. It does not quantify a price adjustment. Auction motives differ from voluntary sales; a low reserve cannot prove voluntary buyer depth. Matched motive evidence remains absent.

### G3 - Project quality

An established management reputation remains a dated positive lead, not a 2026 financial audit. Lift/capex needs, collection, reserves and upcoming levies are unknown. Competitors include other Kiara high-rise units first, and Desa Kiara high-rise stock as a conditional cheaper housing-task alternative. Kiaramas or Desa ParkCity branding is not a direct price anchor. Courtyard stock is diagnostic only and outside the selected high-rise universe.

Dominant roles: current governance is a **Gate**; recurring location utility a **Driver/Floor**; ageing obligations a potential **Ceiling**. Positive location features cannot average away a service-continuity failure.

### G4 - Unit quality

Prefer two legally attached, usable bays **at an affordable all-in basis**, with an acceptable quiet stack and lift route. K1's one-bay disadvantage remains. K2's higher floor and extra bay are claims, not evidence of a superior full package; view, noise, refurb and bay ownership remain unknown. KA's exact address improves identity, but condition, occupancy and actual obligations remain unresolved.

No amount of interior renovation manufactures another legal bay or changes permanent adjacency. Extra rented parking, if available, would support day-to-day use but is not automatically a transferable exit benefit.

### G5 - Price / mispricing

Blocked by G0 and unresolved G3/G4. Do not interpret the RM500k asking difference between K1 and K2 as the value of one bay. Floors, sellers, condition and aspirational asks differ.

At KA, RM558k reserve plus assumed RM200k liability plus 5% acquisition allowance plus RM50k refurbishment produces **RM835.9k total acquisition basis before financing carry**, already close to K1's price before its own costs. It could still be a different deal, but the reserve alone gives no alpha. Legal liability allocation may reverse this result; it has not been established.

### G6 - Exit architecture

The exit buyer needs a usable family home and financeability, not the story that today's bidder purchased cheaply. Current reference set: matched Kiara high-rise units; future set: those units after further ageing and the substitutes available at the buyer's total budget. Market floor and ceiling remain unknown. No measured marketing-duration distribution exists.

Under K1 assumptions, five-year nominal break-even sale is **RM965k**. That is a required price, not evidence a buyer will pay it. If owner demand weakens, an assumed RM3,200 rental does not by itself prove capital recovery. Rent, management and resale are partly dependent supports, not three independent exits. No redevelopment optionality credited.

### G7 - Financing / terminal risk

K1 leaves RM168.5k after entry and the joint stress, only RM18.5k above the protected reserve. Its RM50k-levy sensitivity breaches the reserve. K2 leaves only RM17.7k. KA with the assumed inherited debt leaves RM57.4k. Loan funding of inherited charges is not assumed.

At the past reserve, KA's maximum additional cash-paid liability before the reserve fails is approximately **RM107.4k**, holding all other assumptions fixed. This gives an explicit reversal threshold. It does not establish whether a creditor can collect a particular debt from a buyer.

### G8 - Portfolio / opportunity cost

K2's cash-only acquisition-price ceiling is approximately RM915k under its scenario, far below RM1.35m. This is a mandate ceiling, not a valuation. Paying for parking consumes too much optionality here. K1 survives base stress narrowly and loses to Sterling/Aster on capital flexibility; that allocation comparison is independent of whether TTDI has the stronger address.

K1 plus any of the other three main expressions fails the protected-reserve test. No joint acquisition is authorised by these numbers.

### G9 - Capital decision

**K1 Defer. K2 Preserve Capacity under the synthetic mandate. KA Defer; conditional Preserve Capacity with RM200k liability.** Confidence: high in the arithmetic, moderate in the parking concern, low in matched value/current governance.

Reversal: matched normalised sales and satisfactory current management/unit documents, with a suitable all-in price; KA additionally needs current auction status, actual sale terms and a fully allocated liability ledger. Kill: unremedied service-continuity failure, unacceptable permanent unit penalty, unfinanceable title, or required cash below reserve. These are prospective triggers, not established present defects.

Underwriting sentence: **I do not buy the original RM850k offer yet; the future TTDI high-rise family buyer and cost-inclusive exit have not been evidenced sufficiently, and the newly found two-bay/auction alternatives do not automatically improve the capital decision.**

## P7-03 - Aster Residence, Amber Homes continuation

### G0 - Reference-price validity

**Unresolved; no validated discount.** Keep A1 RM685k from Batch 01. A2 is a separately advertised RM650k low-floor west-facing 953 sf unit (B2-A06), not a verified repricing of A1. AB is a concrete 810 sf RM540k asking alternative (B2-A05). C/C1 asks and duplex asks cannot be pooled.

The apparent 807 sf RM520k transaction lead comes from a page with mixed developer/tenure/location identities (B2-A09); quarantine it pending original scheme mapping. A RM570k 953 sf item is explicitly a listing, not a sale (B2-A10). No family-layout normalised clearing price established. Very low duplex offers remain counter-leads rather than being silently removed because they challenge the preferred thesis.

### G1 - Market mechanism

Existing transport access can support commuters, including small families. Household suitability can coexist with overall investment-oriented positioning; this refinement was supplied by the user before Batch 02. A stronger owner premium requires buyers to pay for the actual living package after completion, not merely the MRT connection.

Lifecycle: completed secondary-conversion test. Usage utility has a plausible mechanism; ownership premium and future repricing remain hypotheses. A future catalyst is not required in the model, but current access does not guarantee appreciation.

### G2 - Buyer universe

Primary for C/C1: commuting couples/young families wanting three genuine rooms and a single-level home. Secondary: organic whole-unit investors. False borrowed buyer: every Cheras landed family, or every MRT tenant treated as a future premium-paying owner. B can serve a tighter-budget small family; duplex serves a narrower set comfortable with split-level sleeping arrangements.

Room-rental demand may explain use but cannot be added up into a guaranteed whole-unit family rent. No transaction-motive or owner-occupation share established. Transfer restrictions advertised for A2 are a document/eligibility question, not a basis for inferring ethnicity or intrinsic desirability.

### G3 - Project quality

Do not label all units investor-only. Official layouts establish capability, while actual density, lift operations, rules, arrears and enforcement determine whether that capability survives occupancy. Current management financial health and family resale conversion remain unverified.

Primary alternatives: other Aster layouts and the Annex/Maxim Residences where the same buyer accepts different space/access tradeoffs. Majestic Maxim is a relevant budget/utility challenger; The Connaught One is a future-supply/package diagnostic until timing and effective pricing match the housing task. No radius-based sum of nearby units is treated as interchangeable supply.

Roles: functional family space and low-friction commuting are **Drivers**; management and irreversible exposure are **Gates**; cheaper usable family alternatives create a **Ceiling**.

### G4 - Unit quality

The official plans were visually inspected (B2-A02-A04). **C/C1 is my preferred family format**: single-level living, balcony and three bathrooms. B is a credible compact household product, but sacrifices spatial separation and balcony. C versus C1 still requires actual stack and entrance/privacy checks; no universal winner between them.

Duplex D has its master bedroom downstairs and two bedrooms above. A household with an older adult downstairs might benefit, while parents supervising young children may dislike separated sleeping floors. Stairs and circulation mean nominal extra area is not a direct usable-space advantage. The diagram is not proof of actual dimensions or ventilation.

Within C/C1, prefer quiet exposure and workable legal bay geometry over renovation. A2's low-floor/west-facing claims require afternoon heat, road/rail noise and privacy assessment; they are not automatically a hard failure. B's advertised two bays do not prove side-by-side parking.

### G5 - Price / mispricing

Blocked. **Better layout is not permission to pay any family premium.** A1 costs RM145k more than AB; A2 RM110k more. At the common financing assumptions, extra mortgage carry is about RM554/RM420 per month, plus the assumed RM50 fee difference. This translates the choice into an actual household tradeoff.

For a constrained investment entry, **investigate B at RM540k ahead of the original C at RM685k**, unless C's quieter exposure, bay arrangement and realised family demand justify its premium. For household utility at similar effective prices and condition, prefer C/C1. These are different objectives, not contradictory rankings.

The RM750k duplex example is unattractive as a default family-investment expression when lower asks remain unresolved. No justified entry band is fabricated; the observed B/C asking ladder is an investigation range only.

### G6 - Exit architecture

C's exit needs a buyer who values the incremental household utility enough to pay over B and nearby substitutes. The B buyer may be more quantum-constrained, but lower quantum alone does not prove broader qualified depth. Market floor/ceiling and sale duration remain unmeasured.

A1/A2/AB five-year nominal break-even sales are approximately **RM745k/RM702k/RM585k**. These are model hurdles, not future sale forecasts. Primary fallback: whole-unit tenancy, conditional on achieved rent. Borrowing capacity and leasing both deteriorate in a common credit/employment shock, so do not double-count them as fully independent protection.

### G7 - Financing / terminal risk

Protected cash after entry and joint shock: A1 RM248.3k; A2 RM258.9k; AB RM291.1k. All pass this one calculation. None has verified lease expiry or actual bank terms. A thirty-year initial loan does not prove the next buyer can obtain the same tenure or LTV five years later.

AB's lower price creates more buffer, but adverse layout conversion, weak management or an undesirable stack can still prevent deployment. Lower asking price does not heal permanent disadvantages.

### G8 - Portfolio / opportunity cost

If comparable daily utility is sufficient for the intended buyer, AB releases roughly RM42.8k more cash after entry/stress than A1 in this model. C must earn the additional commitment through utility and resale acceptance. No increase in household income or borrowing capacity is assumed to accommodate it.

A1 paired with Sterling or Venice fails the reserve. Same-corridor diversification claims would also overstate independence. Do not stack small purchases merely because each looks affordable alone.

### G9 - Capital decision

**Defer** for A1 and the analysed alternatives. Product preference C/C1; capital-efficient verification priority AB. Confidence: moderate in layout capability, low in realised family premium and unit value.

Reversal: attributable same-layout arm's-length sales/credible effective offers, current governance, title and quiet-stack/bay verification, followed by a return case that works at a conservative exit. Kill: proven permanent exposure the target buyer will not accept, materially restricted finance/transfer contrary to the assumed exit, or unresolved critical management failure. A more attractive layout does not waive these gates.

Underwriting sentence: **I would seek an intact three-bedroom Aster for an actual commuting household, but I will not pay the C/C1 premium until resale behaviour and the chosen stack justify it; the RM540k B offer is the current lower-cost expression to investigate.**

## P7-05 - Sterling, Kelana Jaya

### G0 - Reference-price validity

**Unresolved, with useful matching leads.** Selected S1: RM608k, 1,442 sf, advertised 3+1 rooms and two bays (B2-S01). Nearby-size portal records of RM605k/RM670k are leads; duplicate rows, differing areas and unknown parking/condition prevent normalisation. The public page's discount-to-median label is not adopted. An indexed Brickz summary has a different time window/alias and inaccessible underlying records (B2-S02).

At this price S1 is near the lower cited transaction lead, not demonstrably distressed. The proper statement is **plausibly worth underwriting at this quantum**, not “validated bargain.” Current title expiry, actual sale status and motive remain unknown.

### G1 - Market mechanism

Interpretation: recurring PJ employment, services and local household utility can support ownership of a sufficiently good family condo without conferring aspirational-address pricing. The older stock's role is usable space and replacement convenience, not a newness premium or a speculative district rerating.

Lifecycle: mature utility candidate. Price recognition remains unresolved; no growth premium is inserted to make the model profitable. Existing Kelana Jaya calibration supports separating ownership demand from ownership prestige, but is not new independent validation.

### G2 - Buyer universe

Primary hypothesis: working/local households seeking three usable bedrooms, additional flexible space and two bays around this price segment. Secondary: ordinary long-term landlords. False borrowed buyer: all wealthy PJ landed residents, all office workers or all train users assumed to buy this unit.

Same-weekend set: Kelana Mahkota first, other Sterling configurations next; Kelana Puteri/Zenith are secondary leads requiring proper housing-task matching. No proven buyer-motive distribution. Advertised “3+1” does not establish four legal bedrooms.

### G3 - Project quality

Research preference is conditional on actual upkeep. A mature family-sized format has plausible resilience, but old elevators, water systems, waterproofing and lift/service costs can consume the apparent entry advantage. No current management accounts, inspection or committed-capex schedule obtained.

Two bays are a **Gate** for the selected two-car buyer, not a universal rule. Useful layout/local routine are **Drivers/Floors**; management is a **Gate**; competing family condos and ageing/lease financeability are **Ceilings**. Occupancy and a historic fee-collection award, if found, do not substitute for present solvency.

### G4 - Unit quality

Require original floor plan, stack/floor, road exposure, wet-area defects and bay entitlement/geometry before calling S1 superior. A different RM550k Sterling unit is smaller with one bay in its listing body (B2-S07); it is not a like-for-like cheaper substitute. A facilities-floor unit with special outdoor space is also a separate product (B2-S06).

A two-bay family preference can be reversed by a noisy stack or serious water damage. Renovation cost is assumed at RM40k, not quoted. S1's unit number is not known; the analysis remains tied to a specific advertisement rather than inventing an inspectable unit.

### G5 - Price / mispricing

Blocked pending matched evidence. **Kelana Mahkota is the strongest price counterexample.** Its two-bay 1,260 sf corner offer has RM513k header/RM533k body (B2-S08). S1 asks RM75k-95k more for advertised extra space/product differences, before cost normalisation. That is roughly RM286-363 additional monthly mortgage at the base assumptions.

I initially favour S1 for the intended family format, **but the preference is weak enough to reverse** if Mahkota's genuine layout, bays, condition and governance are comparable. A separate Mahkota unit's added partition is not a full-bedroom upgrade (B2-S09). A crossed-out voluntary ask replaced by a one-bay auction is excluded (B2-S10).

### G6 - Exit architecture

Future buyer: the same budget-conscious PJ household, comparing actual functioning family condos after another five years of ageing. Current/future reference set must include Mahkota at the relevant total budget, not premium PJ landed values. No demonstrated liquidity floor or sale-duration estimate.

Using assumed RM2,500 monthly rent and RM500 monthly fees/other costs, S1's nominal five-year break-even sale is **RM684k**, versus RM608k entry. A flat-price exit loses about **RM74k**. This is not capital preservation merely because the cash reserve survives. Return relies on a better negotiated basis, stronger evidenced rental, demonstrable future value, or some combination; none is assumed as proven.

### G7 - Financing / terminal risk

S1 leaves **RM250.9k** after entry plus joint shock and passes all four modelled single-variable sensitivities. That is useful capital resilience, conditional on costs and finance. Lease expiry must be read from title; completion age is not a substitute. Future 70%-LTV/20-year and 80%-LTV/25-year scenarios expose equity and affordability burdens without predicting actual lender policy.

RM0.18 per sf from the sale page is not accepted as a current complete fee bill. The model uses RM350 monthly service/sinking allowance, informed by a different-unit RM344 claim, plus RM150 other costs. Unexpected capex or higher charges can materially weaken the role.

### G8 - Portfolio / opportunity cost

S1 is the first investigation priority because a recognisable household task and manageable quantum coexist, not because it has passed every gate. Its cash resilience is materially better than K1's. Its best opportunity-cost challenge is nearby lower-cost Mahkota, not a disconnected cheaper market.

S1 plus V1 still leaves only RM96.3k protected cash after both shocks, below RM150k. Cheap diversification does not satisfy the locked mandate. Actual existing-portfolio suitability is unknown.

### G9 - Capital decision

**Defer**, with highest verification priority. Initial prospective Deploy expectation not achieved. Confidence: moderate in the utility hypothesis, low in exact value, current governance and clearing motive.

Reversal: S1 genuinely available, legal two-bay and functional layout verified; current governance/capex and title financeability clear; matched clearing and a conservative total-return case support the final effective price. Preference switches to Mahkota if its full package is comparable and its effective price advantage survives verification. Kill: unremediated service failure, major unfunded obligations incompatible with the reserve, non-transferable assumed bay or permanent exposure defeating the family housing task.

Underwriting sentence: **I would investigate buying S1 from a voluntary seller near the advertised RM608k only because the family utility could survive ageing; I cannot yet evidence the price or a future buyer paying the approximately RM684k model break-even.**

## P7-06 - Venice Hill Tower 10

### G0 - Reference-price validity

**Unresolved.** V1 is the advertised RM280k, 1,480 sf Tower 10 unit with one bay (B2-V01). Project-wide records and offers from Towers 5/9 are not its clearing price. Full-loan/zero-down marketing on another Tower 10 unit triggers effective-price and transaction-motive checks (B2-V06); it does not prove financial engineering or misconduct.

No discount to launch, neighbouring expensive stock or a portal median is claimed. A large home at a low PSF can still be expensive relative to its risks and target buyer's alternatives.

### G1 - Market mechanism

Interpretation: the plausible demand source is low absolute cost for generous household space within the wider Cheras daily-life catchment. It is not proven prestige demand, walkable TOD or inevitable recovery from stigma. The selected role would be budget utility, with special-situation upside only after evidence a priced penalty is reversible.

Lifecycle may include ageing/repair and tower divergence; current value recognition remains unresolved. Neither “cheap freehold” nor “bad reputation” supplies the complete mechanism.

### G2 - Buyer universe

Primary hypothesis: budget-limited local households accepting older stock, car-based routines and one bay. Secondary: organic long-term landlord. False borrowed buyer: buyers of newer, well-operated Cheras condos presumed to move across for extra area alone.

One-car households can exist, so a one-bay unit is not universally unsellable. More affluent family preferences cannot be imposed on every price segment. Actual tenancy and buyer motives remain unknown, and resident ethnicity is excluded from quality assessment.

### G3 - Project quality

The 2024 lift report is genuinely adverse but **does not prove the selected Tower 10 currently fails**. The full clipping concerns nine towers without naming each one; funding claims are contested (B2-V02). A contractor lists a completed Venice Hill modernisation without a date/tower (B2-V03). The latter neither cures the historical concern nor may be ignored.

A current structural Reject needs affirmative service/safety/governance evidence applicable to this tower and unit. Low research confidence is not the same as that evidence. The primary **Gate** is service continuity/current governance; size/freehold are potential **Modifiers/Floors**, incapable of cancelling a failed safety gate.

### G4 - Unit quality

V1 floor/stack remain unknown. Inspect lift dependence, access from parking, water ingress, natural light, privacy and slope/drainage exposure without assuming defects exist. Actual 3+1 configuration must be verified. A ground-floor Tower 10 rental advertisement with same-level parking is a concrete counterexample to the claim every household must depend on lifts daily (B2-V05); it may instead trade into water, privacy or security exposure.

Prefer the unit whose necessary daily access remains usable, not mechanically the highest floor or largest advertised area. One bay may constrain some families but is not enough on its own to impose Reject at this budget.

### G5 - Price / mispricing

Blocked. A manageable absolute quantum leaves room to investigate, but the costs are lumpy. The assumed RM40k refurbishment is 14.3% of entry price; a RM30k levy is another 10.7%. These are stress assumptions, not known assessments. A modestly cheaper purchase cannot repair a collective governance impasse by itself.

The genuine counter-thesis is a functioning, separately managed/operated tower with a usable unit and organic budget-family demand. This would defeat a blanket stigma-based rejection. Its factual requirements remain unverified.

### G6 - Exit architecture

Future reference set: other verified Tower 10 units first; nearby budget flats/condos as alternative housing tasks, with tower differences retained. Newer premium Cheras developments are not rerating targets. No established price floor, cap or sale duration.

Model rent RM1,400/month is an assumption informed by an imperfect larger ground-floor RM1,600 ask, not achieved tenancy. Five-year nominal break-even exit is **RM340k**; flat exit loses **RM58.2k**. A displayed 6% gross yield therefore does not establish an Income Engine. Owner and landlord exits both depend on functioning services; they are not independent of the dominant risk.

### G7 - Financing / terminal risk

V1 leaves **RM345.5k** after entry and the prescribed shock. The strong reserve result is real within the model, but it cannot fund away an indefinite service interruption or force collective repair. Freehold advertising does not prove acceptable bank valuation or loanability for this unit.

Test actual bank valuation, building condition, arrears allocation and planned levies. If a verified failure leaves no workable remediation and destroys residential use/financeability, a structural Reject can apply even while G0 remains unresolved. This trigger is not claimed to have occurred.

### G8 - Portfolio / opportunity cost

Lower absolute entry cash is outweighed in research priority by unresolved tower-level service evidence and thinner support for the user's owner-quality objective. That is a prioritisation judgement, not a fabricated probability of failure. With a functioning tower and genuinely better risk-adjusted entry, the ranking must change.

Every tested pair involving V1 fails the protected-reserve threshold. Retain capacity rather than add it as a supposedly cheap second purchase. No verified correlation benefit is claimed.

### G9 - Capital decision

**Defer**, lower priority. The prospective structural-Reject expectation is withdrawn for this selected expression. Confidence: high that present evidence is insufficient for the blanket claim; low about actual current tower quality.

Reversal toward Deploy requires tower-specific operations/capex documents, usable unit and bank terms, organic clearing/tenancy evidence and an adequate all-in return case. Reversal to Reject requires positive current evidence of an applicable unremediable or unacceptably persistent critical failure. Neither advertising reassurance nor an old adverse headline meets those thresholds.

Underwriting sentence: **I do not buy V1 merely for its low quantum, and I do not reject Tower 10 merely from a project-wide historical headline; the investable question is whether this tower can deliver durable, financeable budget-family housing after all costs.**
